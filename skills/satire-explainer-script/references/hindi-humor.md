# Hindi / Hinglish Humor (adult, not slapstick)

Built from the reels the user picked (analyzed by Gemini, JSON in `D:\Regent Scale\youtube\research\hindi-memes\`) plus an evergreen Indian reference bank. English devices in joke-mechanics.md still apply. This file adds what is native to Hindi.

**What "adult" means here:** sharp, implied, observational and a little dark. The audience is trusted to get the joke. Innuendo is suggested, never spelled out ("samajhdaar ko ishaara kaafi"). It is not crude for shock, and it is never sexual about anyone's family. That exact line is what blew up India's Got Latent in 2025.

## H1. Caption vs cinema (scale mismatch)
A small, real situation answered by an iconic overdramatic film moment.
- "Papa abhi bas Pre clear hua hai, kisi ko batana mat", then Vinod Khanna in *Wanted* roaring the son's full IPS credentials. (itsyoursarkaribabu, 7.5/10)
- **Why it works:** recognition (Indian parents) plus a status escalation you can see. The film does the punchline.
- **In an explainer:** "Mera portfolio 2% up hai", then a hero-entry slow motion. Use 2 to 4 second beats only.
- **Rights warning:** film clips trigger Content ID on long-form. Recreate the scene (actor, AI image, voice impression, still frame plus text) instead of ripping it. The reference carries the joke, not the footage.

## H2. The overnight expert (Dunning-Kruger character)
Someone two days into a thing talks like a 30-year veteran.
- "2 days after joining NCC on a date": "Kya lenge aap?" "Indira ji ne bhi yahi sawal kiya tha... goliyan, barood aur zakhm... '71 ke war mein, Lahore se 20 km door." (heyhoneyimfunny, 8.2/10)
- **In an explainer:** a recurring character who interrupts the narrator. "Ek finance reel dekh ke Harshad Mehta", "Gym ke teesre din creatine pe PhD", "WhatsApp University ke Dean". The narrator corrects him with the real data, so the character delivers the myth and the narrator busts it. This is the best fit for infotainment of everything in this file.

## H3. The sarkari escalation list
An impossible list of requirements that ends in arbitrary rejection.
- Brahmanandam as a clerk: birth certificate from the MRO, disability certificate from the government hospital, post it all, "kagaz mil gaye toh phaad dunga". (latteandcappuccino4u, 8.5/10)
- **Why it works:** shared institutional trauma. It is a Hindi-native version of the stinger list (device 2).
- **In an explainer:** insurance claims, KYC, a loan file, a hospital bill, PF withdrawal. Real procedure, listed deadpan, then the real rejection rate as the punchline (a fact, not a joke).

## H4. Lofty setup, ground reality
A poetic or shayari line set against an unglamorous Indian street truth.
- "Mardana kamzori se rangi hui hain shehar ki deewarein, aur tum kehte ho [aurat kamzor hai]", over Ryan Gosling "literally me" edits and Haryanvi music. (prxctmol, 8.5/10)
- **Why it works:** adult innuendo delivered as social observation. Three registers collide (shayari text, Hollywood sigma aesthetic, Haryanvi beat).
- **In an explainer:** open with filmi gravitas, land on a civic fact. "Desh mein gareebi hai, par shaadi ka lawn 2 saal advance mein full hai." This reel is itself a health-infotainment topic: India's quack sex-clinic economy.

## H5. Register clash
Shuddh Hindi or news-anchor gravity for petty things, or the reverse.
- Doordarshan-anchor voice reading a gym bro's diet. A shayari intro for an EMI calculation. A Haryanvi or bhojpuri beat under an existential visual.
- Code-switching is a punchline tool: switch language exactly on the twist word.

## H6. Institutional recognition
Jokes that only work because every Indian has lived them: UPSC parents, 75% attendance, Sharma ji ka beta, relatives at weddings asking for your salary, the office "quick call", the society WhatsApp group, a medical rep's free pen.
- Rule: name the institution in the first 2 seconds. Recognition is the hook.

## H7. Bravado, then the karma cut
Loud overconfidence, a hard cut, and the same person humbled, with a sad or ironic song underneath.
- Two reel creators shouting abuse at the camera, cut to a police station, a cop dryly asking "Mehak kaun hai? Pari kaun hai?", over "Apni to jaise taise..." (vicky926059, 8.5/10)
- **Why it works:** status reversal plus catharsis. The contrast between the street swearing and the flat official Hindi is the punchline.
- **In an explainer:** the overnight-expert character (H2) screams "F&O se crorepati!", hard cut to the real SEBI statistic (about 9 in 10 retail F&O traders lose money; verify the latest SEBI study before using it) over a sad song. Use our own character, never real private people.

## Hindi-specific rules
1. **One joke style per beat, three per minute,** mixing English devices (scale translation, understatement) with H1 to H6.
2. **Swearing is punctuation, not the joke.** Keep strong profanity out of the first 30 seconds and the title and thumbnail, or YouTube limits ads. Brand deals (protein, fintech) want ad-safe videos.
3. **Never:** religion, caste, region-as-insult, sexual jokes about family, real private individuals.
4. **Write in Roman Hinglish** the way people text. Devanagari only for on-screen shayari or formal-register gags (H4, H5).
5. **The viewer is a 20-something in a city, in 2026.** Every reference must be something they own, use or saw this year: Lifebuoy, Nivea tin, Instagram reels, Nykaa sale, hostel bathroom. Not village or grandmother-era things (ubtan, aangan, chulha) unless the joke is that it is outdated.
6. **No "mummy" by default.** Family nostalgia ("mummy ke haath ka...", "Sunday morning") makes it sound like a kids' channel or a Dabur ad. Use mummy at most once per video, and only as a sharp beat (she is right, or she is roasting you), never as warm framing.
7. **The journey starts with YOU doing something mildly stupid on your own,** not with someone lovingly doing it to you. Jackpockets' viewer buys the Casio himself.
