# Hindi Adaptation and Legal Guardrails (India)

Not legal advice. Have a media lawyer review the first 3 scripts.

## What transfers and what does not
| Device | Transfers to Hindi? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Mock-honorific | Yes, strongly | Hindi formal register ("aadarniya", "shriman", "udyogpati") against the street register is a natural comedy gap |
| Stinger list | Yes | Rhythm-based, language-neutral |
| Flat understatement | Yes | Deliver it in a flat Hinglish voice |
| "In unrelated news" | Yes | "Bilkul alag khabar mein..." |
| Idiom gone literal | Rebuild from scratch | English puns die in translation. Use muhavare and filmi dialogue instead |
| Clip as punchline | Yes, but the rights are harder | See copyright below |
| Pop references | Swap the set | Harry Potter and Thanos become Bollywood, cricket, Shark Tank India, Bigg Boss, UPSC, arranged-marriage culture |
| Broke narrator | Yes, very strongly | The middle-class Indian lens ("EMI", "Sharma ji ka beta", "salary credited") is a richer vein than the American "broke guy" |

**Recommended register:** Hinglish (Devanagari-free scripts, natural code-switching). Pure Hindi narrows the audience and loses brand and finance terms.

## Legal: India is stricter than the US
1. **Defamation is criminal in India** (Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, section 356), and it is also civil. Indian billionaires and film stars do sue, and courts grant pre-publication injunctions. The joke "Larry Page performs experiments on children" works in the US. "[Indian tycoon] does X" can get a takedown order plus a case.
   - Rule: stick to facts that are public record and court-reported. Present allegations as allegations, with a source. Use the "in unrelated news" juxtaposition rather than direct claims. Make hyperbole obviously absurd, so that no reasonable viewer reads it as fact.
2. **Religion, caste, region, community:** zero shock tags. Sections 196 and 299 of the BNS cover promoting enmity and outraging religious feelings. The Pope segment works in the US. A godman equivalent in India is a real risk; leave it out unless the facts are fully court-documented.
3. **Politicians and the government:** high risk with low upside for a new channel. His Trump and Xi jokes have no safe Indian equivalent at launch.
4. **Copyright:** India has no broad US-style fair use. Section 52 "fair dealing" covers criticism, review and reporting of current events, which is narrower. News channel and film clips draw Content ID claims and strikes. Keep clips short (under 5 seconds), transformative and commented on. Prefer public-domain material, government footage, your own stock licenses or AI recreations labeled as such.
5. **Brand names in thumbnails** (luxury cars, watches) are fine for commentary. Do not imply sponsorship.
6. **IT Rules 2021 grievance takedowns:** expect complaints. Keep a source file per script so you can respond within the 72-hour window.

## Punch-direction rule (non-negotiable)
Punch up: billionaires, corporations, status games, marketing myths, your own middle-class anxieties.
Never punch down: poverty, ethnicity, religion, caste, disability, victims.
