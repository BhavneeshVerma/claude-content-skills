# Health and Finance Infotainment: India Rules

Checked 2026-11-05 from secondary sources (law-firm and trade-press summaries). Read the primary circulars before monetizing. Not legal advice.

## Finance (SEBI)
- **No stock recommendations, direct or indirect,** unless the speaker is SEBI-registered (an investment adviser or research analyst). "This stock looks cheap" counts.
- **No live or recent market data in educational content.** Per summaries of SEBI's January 2025 circular, data used in education should be at least 3 months old.
- **No performance claims or return promises** from unregistered people.
- **Brand deals:** SEBI-regulated entities (brokers, AMCs, advisers) are restricted from associating with unregistered finfluencers. Brokers as sponsors are therefore mostly off the table. Banking, insurance, credit-card, fintech-app and budgeting-tool sponsors fall under other regulators and remain possible, but check each one.
- **Enforcement is real:** December 2025, Avadhut Sathe's trading academy (Rs 546 crore impounded) for advice branded as education.

**Safe lane for this format:** economics and history explainers ("How Ambani's Jio killed 4 telecoms", "Where your EMI interest actually goes", "What happens if India's AI boom pops"), scale translation, sector stories, personal-finance mechanics (how a credit score works, how inflation eats an FD). Talk about systems, not tickers.

## Health (ASCI, CCPA, FSSAI)
- **Qualifications:** ASCI's influencer guidelines (2023, refined by Addendum 2 on April 7, 2025) require a relevant qualification (doctor, dietitian, nutritionist and so on) to give technical health or nutrition advice in an ad, and the qualification must be disclosed.
- **Disclosure:** every paid integration carries a clear, upfront label (for example "Ad" or "Paid partnership"), plus the platform's paid-promotion toggle.
- **Department of Consumer Affairs (August 2023):** endorsers posing as health experts must carry a disclaimer.
- **Protein and supplement deals:** a single ad faces three regulators at once: FSSAI (product claims), ASCI (disclosure, qualification) and CCPA (misleading ads, endorser liability). Never say "cures", "guaranteed" or "doctor recommended" unless the claim is approved.
- **Plan for it:** put a registered dietitian or doctor on the team (co-host, reviewer or credited consultant). That unlocks the protein deals and is also the credibility moat.

## Skincare and looksmaxxing
- **Cosmetics vs drugs:** under India's Drugs and Cosmetics Act a cosmetic may not claim to treat or cure (acne, pigmentation, hair fall). In scripts, say what studies found about an ingredient, not what a product "fixes".
- **Skin-tone rule:** ASCI's guideline on fairness products bars showing darker skin as inferior or unhappy. Satire of *colourism itself* is fine and on-brand. A joke that treats dark skin as the problem is not.
- **Procedures** (peels, lasers, injectables, IV drips) are medical. Name the risks with a source, never a "best clinic", and never doses.
- **Brand-deal fit:** sunscreen, basic skincare and dermatologist-led brands fit the format. Fairness creams and "glow drips" do not.

## Format rules for both niches
1. Every claim has a source (a study, RBI or SEBI data, WHO, ICMR) in `facts.md`.
2. Jokes target systems, marketing, myths and ourselves. Never the viewer's body, illness or poverty.
3. Use a pinned comment or the description for sources and disclaimers ("educational, not advice").
4. Hard no-go topics at launch: stock tips, crypto calls, disease cures, weight-loss promises, anything about a named doctor or brand being harmful without a court or regulator finding.
